**Headquarters:** Washington, D.C.

### Mission

Rewire the U.S. labor market so that all individuals Skilled Through Alternative Routes (STARs) can work, learn, and earn to their full potential. Opportunity@Work exists to increase career opportunities for the more than 70 million adults in the U.S. who do not have a bachelor's degree but are Skilled Through Alternative Routes.

### Vision

Enable at least one million working adults in America to translate their learning into earning over the next decade — generating a $100 billion boost in annual earnings.

## STARs — Skilled Through Alternative Routes

### Definition

STARs are workers aged 25 and older who are active in the labor force, hold a high school diploma, but do not have a bachelor's degree. They have developed their skills through:

- Military service
- Community college
- Certificate programs and bootcamps
- Apprenticeships
- On-the-job experience
- Workforce training programs
- Self-directed learning and other alternative routes

### Scale

- **70+ million** adults in the U.S. qualify as STARs
- STARs represent roughly **50%** of the U.S. workforce
- STARs reflect the country's racial and cultural diversity:
  - **61%** of African American workers
  - **55%** of Hispanic workers
  - **50%** of non-Hispanic white workers
  - **61%** of veteran workers

### STARs Segments

Opportunity@Work categorizes STARs into three groups based on skills-readiness for higher-wage work:

#### Shining STARs
- **3.1–5 million** workers who currently hold high-wage jobs (paying more than $77,000)
- Living proof that a bachelor's degree is not the only route to gain skills for higher wages
- Demonstrate what is possible when employers recognize skills over pedigree

#### Rising STARs
- **33 million** workers in low- and middle-wage jobs
- Already possess skills for jobs in a higher-wage category
- Many have skills for roles paying at least 50% more than their current jobs
- Blocked by the "paper ceiling" from accessing higher-wage opportunities

#### Forming STARs
- **38.7 million** workers in low- and middle-wage jobs
- Have skills for smaller wage gains through more limited pathways
- Training and development opportunities are important to support Forming STARs in building additional job skills

## The Paper Ceiling

### What Is the Paper Ceiling?

The "paper ceiling" is an invisible barrier limiting opportunities for STARs — constructed over the past 30 years through:

- **Unnecessary bachelor's degree requirements** in job postings
- **Outdated hiring technology** that screens out qualified candidates
- **Lack of professional networks** accessible to STARs
- **Managerial bias** — managers with bachelor's degrees overestimate the percentage of the workforce that has a degree, and these misperceptions affect hiring behavior

### Impact by the Numbers

- Between 2000 and 2020, STARs lost **7.4–7.5 million** middle- and high-wage job opportunities
- In 2000, STARs held **54%** of Gateway and Destination jobs; by 2020, that dropped to **46%**
- The wage gap between STARs and workers with bachelor's degrees has **doubled** over the past 40 years
- Adjusted for inflation, STARs now earn **less on average** than they did in 1976
- It takes STARs more than **30 years** of work experience to earn what a college graduate earns on day one of their career
- The average 25-year-old STAR in 1989 started at **$15.66/hour**; by age 55 in 2018, that STAR earned **$21.77/hour** — barely reaching what a bachelor's degree holder earned in 1989

### Racial & Gender Disparities

- Black and Hispanic STARs make lower wages in their origin jobs than white STARs
- They make more job transitions but these transitions are shorter skill distance for smaller wage gains
- Black, Hispanic, and women STARs are underrepresented in pathways that offer mobility
- When STARs make job transitions, they cannot leverage their skills for higher wages the same way degree holders do

## Research & Reports

### Reach for the STARs (Foundational Report)

**Key Findings:**
- The assumption that lower-wage jobs require fewer skills is untrue — enormous skills overlap exists between lower-wage and higher-wage positions
- At least one possible skills-based transition to a higher-wage job exists for every one of the 70+ million STARs
- Millions of STARs have demonstrated skills for jobs paying at least 50% higher than their current roles
- Every STAR segment has skills to perform a different role for at least 10% more in wages

### Navigating with the STARs

**Three Key Findings:**
1. When STARs make job transitions, they cannot leverage their skills for higher wages the way degree holders do
2. STARs comprise the majority of the essential workforce but continue to experience wage decline; promising pathways exist in growing fields (cybersecurity, healthcare, IT support)
3. Black and Hispanic STARs face compounded disadvantages — lower origin wages, shorter skill-distance transitions, and underrepresentation in mobility pathways

### Rise with the STARs

**Key Findings:**
- Documents the impact of degree discrimination on employers and STARs
- 7.4 million good jobs became unavailable to STARs between 2000–2020
- Managers with bachelor's degrees overestimate workforce degree attainment, affecting hiring behavior
- Identifies evidence-based paths forward for skills-first hiring

### Look to the STARs

**Focus:** How employers can find needed talent, transform local and regional labor markets, and boost STAR mobility by embracing a collective approach to skills-based job pathways.

### State of the Paper Ceiling (April 2025 — Latest Report)

**Key Findings:**
- After decades of declining opportunity, the trajectory for STARs is starting to reverse
- STARs have regained nearly **10%** of lost middle- and high-wage jobs — over **750,000 jobs** recovered since 2020
- From 2008–2019: 6.3 million STARs lost access to good-paying jobs (−0.6% per year)
- From 2019–2023: decline slowed to −0.4% per year
- STARs transitioning to higher-paying jobs increased individual wages by more than **$14,000** in 2023
- **10 million STARs** could move to higher-paid jobs by 2030 if skills-first practices become the norm

**STAR Mobility Compass (new framework):**
- Identifies opportunities for STARs based on employer demand
- 30 good-paying jobs for STARs that pay middle-wage and offer access to higher-wage jobs
- Increasing STAR share of hires into these roles could create opportunities for **3.1 million STARs**
- Compensation for valuable skills could raise wages for **5.1 million STARs**
- New-to-world jobs can open opportunities for **1.8 million STARs**

### Unlocking STAR Mobility in the Public Sector

**Focus:** Research on how government employers can adopt skills-first talent strategies to open public-sector jobs to STARs.

### Additional Research

## Programs & Initiatives

### Tear the Paper Ceiling Campaign

**Launched with:** Ad Council
**Coalition:** 200+ corporate and nonprofit partners (originally 60+, growing)
**Impact:**
- 38% of employers now recognize the campaign (up 43% since 2022)
- 83% of these employers are more likely to hire skilled workers without bachelor's degrees than 2–3 years ago
- Over 90 partners have removed degree requirements and adopted skills-first hiring
- **30 states** committed to removing degree barriers from hiring processes
- 6+ billion media impressions
- Over 5 million people responding to the narrative

### Powering STAR Breakthroughs Grants

- **$2 million** in grants awarded to eight organizations
- First-of-its-kind effort to support organizations boosting economic mobility and combatting occupational segregation
- Focus industries: advanced manufacturing, healthcare, technology, skilled trades, tax preparation, food service
- Communities served from Los Angeles, CA to Baton Rouge, LA to Camden, NJ

### Transformers in the Public Sector

- First-of-its-kind cohort led by Opportunity@Work
- **Participating states:** Arizona, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Louisiana
- Leaders collaborate and receive targeted support on skills-first talent strategies over 12 months

### STARs Public Sector Hub

- Nonprofit, nonpartisan digital resource hub and learning community
- For government leaders interested in skills-first talent strategies
- **Anchor partners:** The Volcker Alliance, Partnership for Public Service, Harvard Government Performance Lab, Lead for America, Govern for America, Cicero Institute, Work for America
- **Goal:** 400,000 more public-sector job postings available to STARs by 2025; 1 million by 2027
- Members have collectively opened over **500,000** middle- and high-wage jobs since 2022 to STARs
- **26 states** committed through this hub to removing degree barriers

## Technology & Data Platforms

### STARSight

**Type:** Interactive digital dashboard and data intelligence suite
**Purpose:** Synthesizes national labor market data and STARs data into intuitive, actionable visualizations
**Features:**
- Quantifies opportunity available for workers and employers in a region when STARs participate fully
- Accessible data feeds for inclusive workforce hiring
- Regional STAR dashboards (dev.stellarsight.org)
- Integrated into partner platforms

### STARSight Connect (Launched 2025)

**Predecessor:** Stellarworx (decommissioned October 2024)
**Type:** Online marketplace / talent matching platform
**Features:**
- Connects STARs, employers, and talent developers
- Every job posted without degree requirements
- Re-orients recruiting evaluation to focus on skills, not where they were earned
- AI-powered matching capabilities

**Stellarworx Legacy Stats:**
- Grew from 67 to 150 employer enrollments between 2021–2023 (124% increase)

**Case Studies:**
- **Aon:** Uses STARSight Connect to recruit STARs for their Apprenticeship Program
- **Merge IT:** Sources diverse talent with skills their clients need
- **Iron Bow:** Targets broader, more diverse talent groups; efficiently fills key IT support roles

### Lightcast Partnership (2025)

- STARs insights embedded directly into Lightcast's industry-leading workforce data software
- New filter in Lightcast Analyst platform uses AI and skills intelligence
- Identifies jobs that could be filled by STARs instead of requiring a bachelor's degree
- Reveals qualified, underutilized talent for millions of roles

### SkyHive AI Partnership

- Workforce AI technology powering skills-based matching
- Originally integrated into Stellarworx (2021)
- AI capabilities continue in STARSight Connect
